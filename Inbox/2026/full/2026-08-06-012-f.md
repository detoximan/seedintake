Introduction

Machiavellianism is the third trait of the Dark Triad
(along with Narcissism and Psychopathy). Unlike the
other two, it isn't driven by ego or impulsive emotions.
It is driven by strategy, patience, manipulation, and
power.

Machiavellianism
[3946233747553086121_3946233389714786291.jpg]: UZI PHILOSOPHY

Where did the term come from?

The word Machiavellianism comes from the 16th-
century Italian diplomat and philosopher Niccolo
Machiavelli, author of The Prince. In the 1960s,
psychologists Richard Christie and Florence Geis
adapted his ideas into psychology and described
Machiavellianism as a personality trait.

Unlike Narcissism or Psychopathy, Machiavellianism
is not classified as a personality disorder. It is
considered a personality trait that exists on a
spectrum.

Morality is conditional

Machiavellians do understand morality. The
difference is that they follow moral rules only when
doing so benefits them.

They usually avoid openly breaking social norms

Nature vs. nurture

Machiavellian traits are influenced by both genetics
and environment.Unlike Psychopathy, environmental
factors appear to play a larger role.

Research links higher Machiavellian traits with
experiences such as:

¢ Childhood neglect

* Emotional abuse

¢ Manipulative parenting

¢ Family conflict

* Growing up in unpredictable environments
For some people, manipulation becomes a survival
strategy that later develops into a stable personality
pattern.

A philosophical perspective
Sometimes, a Machiavellian isn't simply born.
Sometimes, they are created. A child repeatedly

exposed to betrayal may learn that trust is
weakness.

A child who grows up where love is conditional may
learn that manipulation is safer than vulnerability.
Over time, the mask becomes permanent. What
began as survival transforms into identity.

Eventually, they stop seeing people as companions
and begin seeing them as opportunities. Power
replaces connection. Control replaces love.

And behind the carefully constructed mask often
remains someone who no longer remembers what
genuine attachment once felt like.
