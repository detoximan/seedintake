Four teachers, one theory, and a scientific experiment that spirals into the most exhilarating midlife crisis ever filmed. Another Round (2020) is Thomas Vinterberg's Oscar-winning Danish masterpiece — a film that asks a dangerously seductive question: what if humans were born with a blood alcohol level slightly too low, and the cure for a gray, deflated life is simply topping it up?

The atmosphere is intoxication in every sense. Mads Mikkelsen delivers a career-defining performance as Martin, a history teacher so numbed by routine that his students stage an intervention and his family barely registers his presence. When he and three colleagues test the theory — maintaining a constant buzz throughout the workday — the film blooms into something extraordinary: lectures come alive, marriages rekindle, and the screen itself seems to breathe again. Then Vinterberg does what great cinematic storytelling demands and follows the experiment past its golden hour into the wreckage, never moralizing, never flinching. The film holds euphoria and tragedy in the same glass, refusing to tell you which one you're drinking. And then there's the ending — a final scene so cathartic, so perfectly earned, that Mikkelsen's dance has already entered film history.

What makes it a must-watch is its honesty. This is a movie about alcohol that's really about being alive, and the difference between existing and living.
